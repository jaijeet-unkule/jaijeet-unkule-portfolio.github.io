Individual Research Project

The Impact of Computer Mediated Communication (CMC) on Language Learning

Overview

This thesis explored how Computer Mediated Communication (CMC) enhances second language acquisition in digital environments. The research examined how text-based and synchronous digital communication tools influence learning outcomes, engagement, and collaboration compared to traditional classroom settings.

The Problem

With the rapid shift toward digital education, a key question emerged:
Is CMC merely a substitute for physical classrooms, or does it create measurable learning advantages?

The research focused on understanding how digital communication platforms impact listening, speaking, reading, and writing development.

My Role

  • Designed and conducted the full research independently
  • Reviewed and synthesized academic literature on CMC and SLA
  • Identified patterns across text-based, audio, and video communication modes
  • Structured findings into actionable conclusions
  • Developed theoretical insights relevant to digital collaboration environments

Approach

The study analyzed prior research on synchronous and asynchronous communication tools including email, chat, blogs, video conferencing, and collaborative platforms. It evaluated how interaction type, task structure, and social presence affect learning performance.

Key dimensions studied:

  • Text vs. Video communication impact
  • Social interaction and vocabulary gains
  • Cross-modality learning effects
  • Peer collaboration and negotiation of meaning

Key Findings

Text-based synchronous communication promotes higher strategic language use and complexity.

Digital collaboration improves vocabulary retention and writing accuracy.

Structured digital interaction enhances speaking confidence and performance.

Technology alone does not guarantee success; alignment between methodology and interaction format is critical.
